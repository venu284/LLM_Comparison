## 5. Route Anything's lookup routes: definitions and reproduction

Sources: arXiv 2611.05275 v1, §5.1 (pp. 10–11), Table 5 (p. 11), Table 10 and Appendix C.2
(p. 27); code `InfRec-Cardinal/route-anything` at `e137f3f`,
`experiments/quality_floor/crb_core.py` and `crb.py`, and `experiments_x/X9_optimal_routing/README.md`.

- **Cell.** A dimension × source pair. §5.1 (p. 10): "On CodeRouterBench, states are the 21
  dimension×source cells". In code, `crb_core.py`: `meta['bench'] = meta.index.str.split('_').str[1]`
  and `meta['cell21'] = meta.dimension + '|' + meta.bench`. The source is the second
  underscore-separated token of the task id (X9 README, Setup: "source = second underscore token
  of `task_id`").
- **Menu.** §5.1 (p. 10): "each state offers 22 candidates: direct dispatch to one of eight
  models, or one of seven primaries followed by Opus 4.6 when the primary's released score, an
  oracle verifier, is below θ ∈ {1, 0.5}". In code, `build_menu` in `crb_core.py`. Values, costs
  and cell frequencies are fitted on the 7,080 probing tasks.
- **CellBest.** Appendix C.2 (p. 27): "CellBest routes each cell to its best probing model". This
  is direct dispatch to the model with the highest mean probing score in the cell.
- **Zero-loss route (ε = 0).** The cheapest policy whose fitted mean value on probing is at least
  the reference's. `crb_core.price_path` lists the per-cell maximizers of v − λc over the
  breakpoints of λ in [0, η★], in increasing λ. `crb.lp_floor` takes the last path policy that
  meets the floor, or the mixture of it and the next one whose fitted value equals the floor
  exactly. C.2 (p. 27): "the zero-loss policy … is therefore also the quality maximizer at its own
  budget and, since κ > 0, the cheapest policy that meets the floor." On CodeRouterBench it
  randomizes in one cell (X9 README, row ε = 0: "algorithm|lcb: Qwen3-Max 0.881 / qwen3.5-plus
  0.119"), and the paper scores the mixture at its expected value.

**Reproduction on their population.** The script ports `primitives`, `ratio_opt`, `price_path`,
`lp_floor` and `realized` from `crb_core.py` / `crb.py` and fits on the 7,080 probing tasks, with
mean released score and the 148 missing runs kept as recorded, as they did. It scores on the 2,919
ID-test tasks against Always-Opus (`ra.reproduction`):

| | Paper | Script |
|---|---|---|
| Efficiency against Opus | 25.5× (25.516) | 25.516 [24.26, 26.77] |
| ΔV, points | −0.44 [−1.75, +0.97] | −0.44 [−1.77, +0.77] |
| Cost, m$ per task | 0.452 | 0.452 |
| Always-Opus, m$ per task | 11.655 | 11.655 |
| Randomizing cell | algorithm\|lcb, Qwen3-Max 0.881 / qwen3.5-plus 0.119 | the same |
| Calls Opus | never (Finding 6) | never; all 21 cells are direct dispatch |
| CellBest minus zero-loss | 0.087 (8.7 points) | 8.66 points |

The point estimates match to the third decimal. The CIs differ slightly because the bootstrap
draws differ (the script uses `crb_core.py`'s seed 20260925 with 1,000 task resamples; Table 10
comes from X9's run). The reproduction passes, so the next section runs on our population.

## 6. The lookup routes against S0.3 on our population

**Setup.** The complete-case headline set (1,907 tasks, 1,701 problems), success = score 1.0. Of
the 21 cells, 10 occur in this set. Routes are fitted on training folds only: 5-fold cross-fitting
by problem, five seeds, seed 0 for the gate. In each fold:

- The **reference** is the best single model on the training folds (Opus in every fold), and its
  cost on the held-out fold is the baseline cost.
- **The menu is direct dispatch only (8 actions).** Route Anything's 14 cascade actions escalate
  on the released score, which R1 forbids for a router. The zero-loss route is fitted at ε = 0
  relative to the reference. A cell with no training tasks would go to the reference; this never
  happened.
- **CellBest:** each cell goes to the model with the highest training acceptance, ties to the
  cheaper one.
- **DimensionBest:** each dimension goes to the model with the highest training acceptance.
- **Cost-aware DimensionBest:** among the models whose training acceptance in the dimension is
  within 2 points of that dimension's best, the one with the lowest mean training cost.

Each route is scored once per task by the fold that held it out. Saving = 1 − Σcost_route /
Σcost_best and acceptance gap = acceptance_route − acceptance_best, each with a paired 95% cluster
bootstrap over problems (1,000 resamples, the same draws for every route). **S0.3 passes** when the
lower CI bound of the saving is ≥ 20% **and** the lower CI bound of the gap is ≥ −2 points.

Seed 0 (`ra.complete_case.routes.*.seed0`), all intervals paired 95% cluster-bootstrap CIs:

| Route | Acceptance [CI] | Cost, m$ per task [CI] | Saving [CI] | Gap, points [CI] | S0.3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Best single model (Opus) | 67.3% [65.0, 69.5] | 5.39 [5.21, 5.58] | n/a | n/a | n/a |
| Zero-loss, direct dispatch | 65.9% [63.7, 67.9] | 2.67 [2.52, 2.83] | 50.5% [47.9, 53.1] | −1.3 [−3.2, +0.5] | **fails** (gap) |
| CellBest | 70.3% [68.3, 72.3] | 4.16 [3.97, 4.33] | 22.9% [20.8, 25.2] | +3.0 [+1.5, +4.5] | **passes** |
| DimensionBest | 69.8% [67.6, 71.7] | 4.84 [4.66, 5.04] | 10.2% [8.9, 11.5] | +2.5 [+1.1, +3.9] | fails (saving) |
| Cost-aware DimensionBest | 69.8% [67.6, 71.7] | 4.84 [4.66, 5.04] | 10.2% [8.9, 11.5] | +2.5 [+1.1, +3.9] | fails (saving) |

Seeds 1 to 4 give the same verdicts. The point estimates range as follows (all five seeds):
zero-loss saving 49.5–51.1%, gap −1.3 to −0.5; CellBest saving 22.4–23.0%, gap +2.5 to +3.1;
DimensionBest saving 9.9–10.2%. Cost-aware DimensionBest picks the same models as DimensionBest in
every fold: in no dimension is a cheaper model within 2 points of the best.

**CellBest passes by a thin margin on cost.** Its saving lower bound is 20.4–20.9% across the five
seeds (20.8% on seed 0), less than one point above the 20% threshold. Its gap lower bound is
comfortable (+0.95 to +1.63). Less than one point of saving separates it from failing, and the
S0.2 price-sensitivity sweep has not been run on it, so the pass should be read as "at the
threshold", not "clear of it". The zero-loss route misses on the other side: its gap lower bound is −2.4 to −3.2
across seeds, while its saving is far above 20%.

The other three cells (algorithm\|bcb 29, code_completion\|bcb 26, data_science\|bcb 12 tasks)
are small. CellBest keeps Opus on cruxeval, code_generation\|bcb and code_generation\|mbpp, and
sends ds1000 to kimi-k2.5 and the HumanEval and MBPP-completion cells to cheaper models. Accuracy
goes up and cost goes down. The zero-loss route goes further: it sends code_generation\|bcb to a
cheaper model on all five seed-0 folds and code_generation\|mbpp on four of them. Its acceptance
is 4.4 points below CellBest's; this recount did not split that difference by cell.

**Caveats.**
- CellBest's cells use the source benchmark, read from the task id. §2 of the plan says the
  source label is not available at decision time. Route Anything reports that cells predicted from
  the prompt nearly match the true ones on its population (Table 11, p. 28: 25.32× against 25.52×).
  That has not been checked on ours.
- Opus passes only 22.0% of code_generation\|mbpp under success = 1.0, far below its other cells.
  That looks like a scoring artefact and belongs in the S1.7 audit. CellBest sends this cell to
  Opus, so the cell does not create CellBest's saving.
- The zero-loss route randomizes in one cell (code_completion\|cruxeval, Opus against gpt-5.4, on
  four of the five seed-0 folds). It is scored at its expected value, as Route Anything does. A
  deployed version has to draw.

**Pass rates for the S0.3 example.** Under success = 1.0 on the headline set: Opus 66.8% (1,971
tasks) and 67.3% (complete case); qwen3.5-plus 41.7% and 42.2%. The next-best model is gpt-5.4 at
52.5% and 53.2%. qwen3.5-plus is not second under this rule.

## 8. Open questions

1. **S0.3 (the user's decision).** As written, S0.3 compares the router with the best single
   model. CellBest, a 10-entry lookup table with no verifier, already passes it on every seed,
   though with a saving lower bound of only 20.4–20.9%. If
   S0.3 stays as it is, passing the performance gate says nothing about whether the router adds
   anything over R2. Requiring the router to beat CellBest at matched acceptance (or to pass S0.3
   with CellBest as the reference) would make the gate measure the router. This document reports
   the evidence and does not decide.
2. **Source labels in CellBest.** CellBest reads the source benchmark from the task id, which §2
   of the plan rules out as a router feature. If the R2 baseline is held to the same rule, it
   needs predicted cells (Route Anything's Table 11 method). Its result on our set is unknown.
3. **Training population.** The routes and the best single model were fitted on the training
   folds of the complete-case headline set, which is the brief's reading. S0.11 says training uses
   the full S0.6 pool and only scoring is restricted to the headline set. The two readings may
   pick different cells and models; S2 should fix one.
4. **Complete case is not a random drop.** The 64 dropped tasks are where glm-5 (mostly) returned
   nothing. S2 still has to fix how a baseline that needs such a cell is scored (S0.11), for
   example by skipping the rung in replay.
5. **code_generation\|mbpp.** Opus at 22.0% and the next model at 11.0% under success = 1.0 is far
   out of line with the other MBPP cell (75–81%). Add it to the S1.7 audit.
6. **Ladder enumeration.** The fixed ladders keep the old simplifications that §4 did not fix:
   point estimates only, cost order taken on the whole population, and only 2- and 3-rung
   cost-ordered permutations.
