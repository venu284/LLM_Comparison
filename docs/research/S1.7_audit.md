## Checkpoint 1 (2026-11-20): what is known without reading a response

### 1. Model mapping (criteria §5.5)

The response matrix has 9,999 ID tasks under 10 keys: the 8 released model names, `通义千问Max`
(9,999 tasks) and `qwen3.6-plus` (1,111 tasks).

- **Byte aliases:** `Qwen3-Max` and `通义千问Max` are byte-identical on all 9,851 tasks where both are
  non-empty (share 1.0). Every other pair shares between 0% and 8.5% identical responses, so no
  other key is a copy of another.
- **Mapped by the rule:** `claude-opus-4-6`, `claude-sonnet-4-6`, `gpt-5.4`, `kimi-k2.5` (own-model
  Spearman ρ 0.969–0.980, the highest in each row), and `Qwen3-Max` (ρ 0.817), with `通义千问Max` as
  its alias.
- **Not mapped by the rule:** `glm-5` (own ρ 0.303; row top `gpt-5.4` 0.800), `MiniMax-M2.7`
  (0.480; top `gpt-5.4` 0.744), `qwen3.5-plus` (0.063; top `gpt-5.4` 0.790). Under §5.5 these three
  released models have **no graded key**, so their cells cannot be read or re-run.
- **Why the rule fails for those three (diagnostic, not a rule):** their visible text is short
  against their released token counts. Median characters per released output token: `glm-5`
  0.35, `qwen3.5-plus` 0.43, `MiniMax-M2.7` 0.64, against 2.6–3.5 for the other models. In no cell
  of the three is the visible text longer than 8 characters per token. This fits output-token
  counts that include reasoning the response matrix does not hold. It does not show the keys are
  wrong, and nothing points to another key: no other key is an alias.
- **Control: the 8-characters-per-token check cannot confirm these three.** Run against every
  key's text (`chars_per_token_control_key_x_model` in `e0_audit.json`), many wrong keys also stay
  under 1% of cells over 8 characters per token:
  - against `glm-5`'s token counts, 4 of 7 other keys pass;
  - against `MiniMax-M2.7`'s, 7 of 7 pass;
  - against `qwen3.5-plus`'s, 5 of 7 pass.

  Their inflated token counts leave room for almost any text. The name-equal key is the only one
  at exactly 0.0% in each of the three columns (the lowest wrong key is 0.02%). That is weak
  support, not confirmation.
- **`qwen3.6-plus`:** covers exactly the 1,111 tasks of `bcb` (675), `mbpp` (225), `bcb_v014`
  (111) and `humaneval` (100), i.e. the code_generation dimension. 520 of its 1,111 responses are
  empty. It is byte-identical to `Qwen3-Max` on 11 of 591 shared non-empty tasks. It has no
  released counterpart under §5.5.
- **Qwen3-Max's token records are constants.** In every one of the 29 families where Qwen3-Max
  has available cells, its released `output_tokens` takes a single value (for example 156 on all
  of `bcb`, `bcb_v014`, `humaneval` and `mbpp`; 226 on `lcb`; 784 on `debugbench` and `swebench`).
  Its ρ of 0.817 therefore measures differences between families, not per-response length. On the
  code_generation tasks the length rule cannot say whether Qwen3-Max's released scores came from
  the `Qwen3-Max` responses or the `qwen3.6-plus` responses. Under the brief ("if this can't be
  settled, carry both keys forward"), both are carried forward for `bcb`, `bcb_v014`, `humaneval`
  and `mbpp`.
- **Which key the released scores were graded from:** for the five mapped models, the name-equal
  key. For Qwen3-Max, `Qwen3-Max` (= `通义千问Max`) outside code_generation; unsettled inside it. For
  glm-5, MiniMax-M2.7 and qwen3.5-plus, not settled by §5.5 (decision A below).

### 7. Decisions for the checkpoint 1 review

- **A. Mapping for glm-5, MiniMax-M2.7, qwen3.5-plus.** §5.5 gives them no graded key, so 3 of the
  8 models (about 30,000 cells) could not be audited at all.
  - Proposed amendment: map a name-equal key that has no alias conflict to its model **by name
    alone**. No score-free check can confirm the mapping for these reasoning models: the length
    correlation fails (§1), and the control shows the characters-per-token check also passes most
    wrong keys. The amendment rests on the key names in the release's own response matrix and on
    the absence of any alias.
  - The alternative is to leave them unmapped. The labels would then rest on five models, and the
    report would say so.
- **B. Qwen3-Max on code_generation.** Both `Qwen3-Max` and `qwen3.6-plus` are carried forward
  for `bcb`, `bcb_v014`, `humaneval` and `mbpp` (brief rule), and both are read and re-run on the
  sampled Qwen3-Max cells there.
  - Outside S1.7, for the reviewer: Qwen3-Max's released `output_tokens`, and so its released cost
    (S0.2), is one constant per family, not a per-response record.
- **C. Empty responses** (brief decision 1): are they unavailable or failures? 895 empty cells
  (name match) carry a released score of 1.0, 850 of them in `lcb`. "Unavailable" would be CLAIMS
  amendment A2 and would change the complete case.
- **D. Suspect scope.** All 20 eligible families except `he_completion` are now suspects:
  - `hex_python` and `mbpp_completion` join on one or two off-rule cells and a flagship below
    the median;
  - the five `bcb*` families, `ds1000` and `humaneval` are in through X12b.

  A family that ends **undecided** leaves the headline, so the headline can shrink a lot. For
  example, if the BigCodeBench sandbox fails §3.4 (more than 10% `our_harness` errors), all 1,097
  BigCodeBench tasks leave it. The flag rules are as pre-registered and have not been changed.
- **E. Stream ids.** S1.7 uses `[0, 100+]`. The plan gives S2.4 `[seed, 2]`, `[seed, 3]`, … with
  no upper bound. Proposed plan text: "S2.4 uses stream ids 2–99; 100 and up belong to S1.7."
- **F. Identical-prompt pairs.** Proposed amendment to §8.2: instead of all 638 differing cells,
  read and re-run a sample of n = 97 of them (stream 129). The 12,080 released comparisons stay
  reported in full.
- **G. Grader.** If the user can open the Wayback snapshot, any grading code found there would
  replace §2's rule by amendment before checkpoint 2.
